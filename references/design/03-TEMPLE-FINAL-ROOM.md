# 03 — Final Room (The Choice)

## The Feeling

You've walked the hall. You've contemplated twenty-two archetypes. Now the temple narrows, quiets, and delivers you to a single room.

In the center: the Choice.

Above: a shaft of light falling from an opening in the ceiling, landing on the Choice.

Nothing else.

## The User's Correction

The prototype had cluttered this room — plinths, lamps, extra platforms, decorative objects. The user said: clean it all out. The final room should *just* be the Choice.

This wasn't a minor note. It was a fundamental correction about what this room *is*. The power of the Choice comes from its solitude. Every extra object dilutes it.

## What Belongs Here

- **The Choice** — the culminating interactive/contemplative element
- **The light shaft** — from a ceiling opening, falling directly onto the Choice. This is the most important visual in the room. It should feel like the temple itself is pointing at this moment.
- **Essential architecture** — walls, floor, the ceiling with its opening. Nothing decorative.

## What Does NOT Belong Here

- Extra platforms (one, not two)
- Lamps, burners, candles — the light shaft is the light source
- Plinths, rings, stubs, pedestals that aren't the Choice itself
- Corner decorations
- Anything archetype-related (that lives in the galleries)
- Anything that makes the eye go anywhere except the Choice

## The Vestibule (If Needed)

If the transition from galleries to final room needs a spatial beat, a small threshold is fine. But:

- It must feel like a **held breath**, not a room
- No stations, no objects, no decoration
- Just architecture and the sense that you're about to arrive somewhere important
- Short. A few steps. Then the final room.

## The Silence

The audio should drop to near-silence here. The narration should build to this moment and then *stop talking*. Let the Choice have the last word by saying nothing.

## Narration

The TEMPLE track ("One Road, Twenty-Two Stations") builds to this moment. The narration should **stop** before or as the user enters the final room. The Choice happens in silence. The voice has guided the entire journey — now it gets out of the way.

## Nuance

The emptiness is the design. This is the hardest room to get right because the instinct is to fill it. Resist. The user's eye goes to the Choice because there's nowhere else to go — and that's the point.
