# 06 — Duat

## The Feeling

The Egyptian underworld. Dark, vast, mysterious — but not scary. Contemplative darkness. You're in a space that feels ancient and deep, lit by pools of golden light on the ground and floating orbs of light in the air.

Above: an eclipse — a black disc ringed with gold. This is the visual anchor of the scene.

## The Mythology

Duat is grounded in the Egyptian afterlife journey — specifically the **twelve hours of Ra's night voyage** through the underworld, drawing from the Book of Gates and the Amduat. The journey includes the weighing of the heart, the solar barque, gate guardians, and the eventual dawn return.

This isn't decorative Egyptology. The twelve-hour structure gives the scene a **narrative spine**: descent into darkness, trials and gates, then return at dawn. The user should feel they're on a journey through the night toward morning.

## The Two Parts

### The Pyramid (Exterior)
A bricked pyramid in the desert beneath an aurora and star-strewn sky. This is the threshold — the monument that marks the entrance to the underworld. It should feel ancient, massive, silhouetted against the night sky.

### The Duat (Interior)
Inside: a **winding blue path** (like a river) cutting through darkness. **Gold-lit gate posts** marking passages. **Floating orbs** of light. A **golden arch** — a major architectural moment. **Gate structures** that feel like thresholds between hours. A **station beacon** — a point of light marking progress.

The blue river/path is the journey line. The gold gates are the hours. The user follows the light through the darkness.

## Entry and Exit

- **Entry:** Through a fade-crossing — the world dissolves and the Duat resolves. Not a door, a transition.
- **Exit:** A **dawn return** path — the user comes back as morning breaks. The journey through night ends in light. This is the emotional payoff.

## The User's Correction

> "It looks boxy no?"

The prototype looked like it was built from boxes. Flat rectangles. Hard edges. It had the right *elements* but they felt like 3D primitives, not a real place. The user saw it immediately.

## What "De-boxed" Means

This isn't about adding more detail. It's about making every shape feel **organic**:

1. **No floating boxes.** The prototype had a brown brick-textured box floating in the upper right. It looked like a mistake. If there's architecture, it should feel carved, weathered, intentional — not like a cube with a texture slapped on.

2. **Doorframes need depth.** The prototype had flat rectangular doorframes — literally rectangles. They need to feel like *passages* — with thickness, with shadow inside, with the sense that you could walk through them into darkness. Bevel the edges. Give them dimension. Make them feel carved from stone, not extruded from a shape tool.

3. **Soften the ground edge.** Where the platform meets the void, the prototype had a hard line — platform, then nothing. This edge needs to dissolve. A gradient into darkness. The platform should feel like it's floating in an endless dark, not sitting on a stage.

4. **The dark mass needs form.** The prototype had a large dark shape (dome/mass) that read as a flat black blob. It needs curvature — the sense of a rounded form in shadow — or a rim of light tracing its edge so you understand its shape. Darkness with form, not darkness as absence.

## What to Preserve (These Are Intentional)

- **Glow pools** — circles of golden light on the ground. These are the path, the warmth in the darkness.
- **Floating orbs** — small lights drifting in the air. They give the darkness life and scale.
- **The eclipse disc** — black circle with a gold halo. This is the centerpiece. It is NOT a bug or a rendering error. It's the most important visual in the scene.

## Narration

No dedicated Duat narration track exists yet in the catalogue. The scene currently relies on atmosphere and the Vision of Creation (file 16) for its storytelling. If narration is added, it should follow the twelve-hour structure — a voice guiding through the night toward dawn.

## Nuance

Duat should feel like the inside of a mystery — dark but not empty, ancient but not dead. The gold-on-black palette is the whole identity. Every light source should feel precious because the darkness is so complete.

The user's eye for "boxy" is the quality bar here. If any element looks like a primitive — a box, a flat plane, a perfect cylinder — it's wrong. Everything should feel touched by time, worn, organic.
