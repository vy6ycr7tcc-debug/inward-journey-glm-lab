# 05 — Tree Station ("The Catalyst of the Body")

## The Story (FROZEN — Do Not Change)

This is a seven-part narrative about breaking and rebirth. It's the most story-driven scene in the series. The beats, in order:

### Beat 1: Full Leaf
A great tree in full, lush leaf. Abundant. Alive. Golden-green canopy, full and soft. This is the "before" — wholeness.

### Beat 2: Dry Season
The canopy browns and thins. Leaves fall. The tree is still standing but diminished. This isn't death — it's the drying out, the hard season. The color shifts from green-gold to brown, sparse.

### Beat 3: Breaking
Branches break. They don't disappear — they **remain as stubs**, with visible notches where they snapped. The tree is bare now, just trunk and broken branches. This should feel painful but honest. The breaks are part of the tree now.

### Beat 4: Emergence
A spirit — a being of light — emerges from **inside the trunk, through the notches**. This is specific and important: the spirit doesn't descend from the sky, doesn't appear from nowhere. It comes *out of the tree itself*, through the wounds. The broken places are where the light gets out.

### Beat 5: Watering
The spirit waters the roots. There should be a visible stream — droplets of light falling to the base of the tree. Tending. Care. The spirit isn't just appearing; it's *nurturing*.

### Beat 6: Rebirth
New branches grow. But — and this is the key nuance — they grow in **different shapes**. This is not restoration. The tree doesn't go back to how it was. It becomes something new. The new growth should look different from the original canopy — different color, different form. Rebirth, not repair.

### Beat 7: The Painting
The spirit paints a luminous image of the tree — **with the broken branches included**. The painting doesn't edit out the damage. The broken places are part of the beauty. The painting should be readable as a tree, luminous, with visible broken branches in the image.

## The Emotional Arc

Wholeness → loss → breaking → emergence → tending → transformation → integration.

The user should feel: this is about what happens when you break and don't go back to who you were, but become someone new who includes the breaking.

## Spatial Nuance (from prototype)

The tree stands on a **slope**. The base of the tree is significantly above where the viewer sits. This caused major problems in the prototype — the tree was buried, the ground was wrong.

**The lesson:** Every element (tree, ground, spirit, painting) must be positioned relative to the *actual terrain height* at its location, not at a flat y=0. The scene exists on a hillside. Build it that way from the start.

**The camera** is at seated eye height, looking slightly up at the tree. The viewer is below, looking up — the tree towers. This power dynamic (small viewer, great tree) is part of the feeling.

## Visual Nuance

- The canopy should be **painterly** — soft, impressionistic, not photorealistic leaves
- Exposure matters: the prototype had blown-out white canopies. The tree should glow golden-green, not blast white.
- The spirit is a **bright orb of light** — simple, not a character with a face
- Fireflies or light motes in the air add life
- The painting (beat 7) should be clearly a *painting* — framed, luminous, slightly stylized — not a photograph

## Narration

**Track:** TREE — "The Catalyst of the Body"
- Female voice (Aria)
- ~4.5 minutes duration
- 73 subtitle cues
- The beats should align with the narration. The story the voice tells and the story the visuals tell are the same story. When the voice speaks of breaking, branches break. When it speaks of the spirit emerging, the light comes through the notches.
