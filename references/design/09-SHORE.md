# 09 — Shore

## The Feeling

Where water meets land. Gentle waves, soft light, the horizon. Contemplative — the shore as a threshold between elements.

## The Problem

**A figure should be present in the composition but wasn't rendering.** The prototype's shore was missing its human element — there should be a figure (a person, a silhouette) that gives the scene scale and emotional focus.

## Why the Figure Matters

Every other scene is about spaces and presences (spirits, holograms, orbs). The shore is where the *human* appears — a silhouette standing at the water's edge, looking out. This is the viewer seeing themselves.

The figure should be:
- **A silhouette** — not detailed, not a character. A shape.
- **Still** — standing, contemplating. Not walking, not doing anything.
- **Small in frame** — the vastness of water and sky should dwarf the figure. The feeling is: one person before the infinite.

## The Scene

Water, sky, horizon. Soft light — dawn or dusk. Gentle waves. The figure stands where the water meets the sand.

The camera should frame this like a painting — the figure placed with intention (off-center, following the eye naturally to the horizon).

## Narration

**Track:** J01 — "The Shore"
- Female voice (Aria)
- 7 subtitle cues
- Short and contemplative. The voice should be sparse — letting the image of the figure at the water's edge carry the meaning.

## What to Avoid

- A detailed human model. This kills the universality. It should be anyone — which means it should be no one in particular.
- An empty shore. Without the figure, it's just a landscape. The figure is what makes it *about* something.
- Dramatic waves or stormy weather. Calm water. The contemplation is in the stillness.
