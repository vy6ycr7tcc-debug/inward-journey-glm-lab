## The Role of Narration

The voice **guides, doesn't lecture**. It offers images and invitations, not explanations. The tone throughout is: "consider this" not "this means that."

The narration and the visuals tell the **same story**. In the tree station, when the voice speaks of breaking, the tree is breaking. When it speaks of rebirth, the new branches are growing. They're synchronized — not just playing at the same time, but *about* the same thing at the same time.

## Subtitle Cues

Every narration track has timed subtitle cues. These must be precise — the text on screen should match what's being said, when it's being said.

The tree station narration ("The Catalyst of the Body") has 73 cues across ~4.5 minutes. This density means the subtitles are almost continuous — the voice is speaking for most of the scene.

## The Silence Principle

The most important narration decision is **when not to narrate**:

- The final room (file 03): narration builds to the Choice, then stops. Silence.
- Transitions between scenes: let the visuals breathe before the voice returns.
- Galaxies (file 11): possibly no narration at all (open decision).

The voice is a guide who knows when to shut up. If the narration is talking over a moment that should be felt, not explained, it's wrong.

## Timing Integrity

When modifying scenes, **do not drift the narration timing**. If a scene gets longer or shorter, the subtitle cues must be re-mapped. A cue that fires 3 seconds late breaks the synchronization between voice and image, and the user will feel it even if they can't name it.
