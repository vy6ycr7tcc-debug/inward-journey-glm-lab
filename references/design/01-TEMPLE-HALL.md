# 01 — Temple Hall

## The Feeling

You enter a golden temple. You're not exploring — you're being *led*. The path moves forward but gently weaves left and right, like slow breathing. With each sway, a new part of the temple reveals itself.

This weaving is the signature of the whole experience. It should feel like the temple itself is breathing with you.

## The Movement

The camera doesn't go straight. It sways — left, right, left — as it moves forward. Gentle. The lateral swing is about a meter each way. Enough to feel it, not enough to feel seasick.

**Why it matters:** The sway means you naturally look left, then right, then left. The archetype galleries (file 02) are on both sides. The weave is how you discover them — not by turning your head, but by the path itself showing you.

**Nuance:** This should feel like walking meditation, not a ride. If the weave is too wide or too fast, it becomes a gimmick. Too subtle and it's just a straight corridor. The prototype found the sweet spot — replicate the *feeling*, not just the numbers.

## The Architecture

Golden stone. Columns on both sides. Warm light from above — think late afternoon sun through high windows.

The columns do compositional work: as you weave, they frame and reveal the gallery stations in rhythm. You're walking through a colonnade where each gap between columns offers a glimpse of something sacred.

**Scale:** Grand enough to feel humbled. Intimate enough to feel held. Not a cavern, not a closet.

## What to Avoid

- A straight corridor. The weave is non-negotiable.
- Clutter. The hall is a passage. Its job is to carry you forward and show you the galleries. It doesn't need decoration.
- Cold light. This is a golden temple. Every light source should feel warm.
- Rushing. The pace is contemplative. The user should want to linger, not hurry through.
