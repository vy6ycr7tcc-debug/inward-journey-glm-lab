# 16 — The Vision of Creation (Narration Animation)

## What This Is

This is the North Star for how narration looks when it's visualized. The user explained this specifically. It's not decoration — it's the core visual language for bringing the spoken words to life.

## The Concept

When narration plays, **luminous points** gather to form imagery **derived from the transcript** — the words being spoken shape what you see. Then the image **dissolves**, and the points **gather into the next form**.

It's a continuous cycle: form → dissolve → reform. The imagery is never static. It's always becoming.

## The Nuance

- The points are **luminous** — they're made of light, not particles of dust
- The imagery comes from the **transcript** — what you see relates to what's being said. Not literal illustrations, but visual echoes of the words.
- The transitions are **dissolves**, not cuts. One image breathes into the next.
- Everything **blends naturally into the surroundings** — the points don't exist in a void. They're part of the scene's atmosphere.
- For dark scenes (like galaxies), the effect can be graded **warmer/golden** — the points carry warmth even in darkness.

## Why It Matters

This is how the game makes narration *visual* instead of just audible. The user isn't just hearing words — they're watching the words become light, become form, become the next form. It's the difference between a voiceover and a vision.

## What to Avoid

- Literal illustrations of the text. This isn't a picture book.
- Static images that pop in and out. Everything dissolves and reforms.
- Points that feel like generic particles. They're luminous, intentional, meaningful.
- Disconnecting the visuals from the words. If the narration speaks of water and the points form something angular and dry, it's wrong.
