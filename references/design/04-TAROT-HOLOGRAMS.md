# 04 — Tarot Holograms

## The User's Correction (exact words)

> "I didn't want the actual copy paste image of the tarot cards but a rendering … that type of holographic"

## What This Means

When the user saw the prototype, the cards looked like someone had taken a photo of a tarot deck and pasted it onto a floating rectangle. Technically it worked. Artistically it was wrong.

What the user wants: the cards should feel like they're **made of light**. Projected. Holographic. When you look at one, you should think "that's a being of light showing me something" — not "that's a picture of a card."

## The Materiality (This Is the Key Nuance)

There are two ways to render a card:

1. **As matter:** An opaque surface with a picture on it. It blocks light. It has edges. It looks like a thing.
2. **As light:** A luminous projection. It *adds* light to the space. You can almost see through it. Its edges glow. It feels present but not physical.

The user wants #2. This isn't a technical preference — it's about what the cards *are* in the world of the temple. They're not objects. They're manifestations.

## How to Achieve It

**The image becomes light:** Take the card imagery and treat it as a light source. The bright parts of the image glow. The dark parts become faint warm haze, not black paper. The whole card is tinted toward temple gold.

**It should have a light-field quality:** At the edges, where the card meets the air, there should be a soft golden rim — like the light is spilling out. When you view the card from an angle, this rim flares. This is what makes it feel holographic rather than flat.

**Subtle scanlines and shimmer:** A very fine, slow-moving line pattern. A gentle flicker — not a strobe, not a glitch. Think of how a projected image breathes. Each card should have its own rhythm so they don't pulse in sync like Christmas lights.

**The ignition:** When a card activates, there's a flare — a bright blooming flash — then it settles into its steady glow. This is the moment the archetype "arrives." It's important. Don't skip it.

**It floats:** Gentle bobbing, subtle sway. Alive but calm. Not bouncing, not spinning.

## What It Should Feel Like

- **Up close:** You're looking at light shaped into an image. You can see the archetype's symbolism. It glows. The edges are soft. There are faint lines moving through it like it's being projected.
- **From across the hall:** A warm golden glow in a niche. You know something sacred is there before you can see what it is.
- **Never:** A photograph. A poster. A screen. Anything that looks printed, pasted, or stuck on.

## The Taste Line

Contemplative hologram, not sci-fi hologram. Think temple, not spaceship. The reference is sacred light — icons, auras, visions — not Star Wars.

If it starts looking like a sci-fi UI element, you've gone too far. Pull it back toward warmth, gold, softness.

## iPhone Reality

Whatever technique is used, it must work on iPhone Safari. The prototype learned that certain texture approaches crash iOS. The hologram must be built with mobile-safe rendering from the start — no exotic texture filtering, no assumptions about desktop GPU features.
