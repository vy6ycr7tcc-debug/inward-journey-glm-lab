Look up on a clear night, far from the city's glare, and the sky does something strange. It stops being a ceiling and becomes a depth. You fall upward into it. And sooner or later, lying in the grass with the cold seeping in, a question rises that no telescope can answer. Are we alone down here.

Cosmic wisdom says we are not. It says the universe is full the way a forest is full, mostly out of sight, mostly going about its own business, and that some of its inhabitants have taken a particular interest in this small, troubled, beautiful world. Not as conquerors. Not as saviors. As something quieter than both.

This is not a new suspicion. More than four hundred years ago, a philosopher was put to death in Rome, charged in part with teaching that the stars were distant suns with their own earths. And long before him, the Maya counted thirteen heavens above the earth and nine underworlds below, each with its own inhabitants, joined by a great tree. Every age has looked up and refused to believe the dark was empty.

There is an old teaching about the wanderers. Now and then, it says, a soul from a much older world volunteers for the hardest assignment there is. It agrees to be born here, a human being, with no memory of where it came from and no proof of why. It will grow up feeling, without knowing why, that it was sent.

The tradition says they arrive in waves, and the waves come when the need is greatest. When the idea of freedom was being born, a wave came. When the world split the atom and held its breath, another wave came. Whether you believe it or not, you have met these people. They are the ones who cannot walk past a stranger's pain. They are the ones who stayed kind in the places that tried to make them cruel.

And here is the strange mercy of the arrangement. The volunteer forgets everything. The same veil of forgetting falls over them as over all of us, because the forgetting is not a punishment. It is the classroom. To choose love while remembering nothing, with no evidence, on faith alone, tempers the soul the way fire tempers iron. An open book test may produce an excellent score, the teaching says, but it teaches nothing. The wanderer gambles everything, even the memory of home, on the chance to be useful in the dark.

But they are not the only ones watching. The same wisdom speaks of a fellowship of older worlds, allied in a single work: to plant seeds of love, seeds of wisdom, seeds of unity in human hearts, and then to wait, the way a gardener waits, for what was planted to decide to grow.

And here is what makes them trustworthy. They will not coerce you. Not even to help you. These presences wait to be called. They will not override your freedom. Your freedom is the whole point of the exercise.

So there are no landings on the White House lawn. When the tradition was asked whether these beings would ever step in and set everything right, the answer was no. Not from indifference. From respect. The fixing is ours to do. They are waiting for us to know it too.

Then how do they help at all. The tradition's answer is so ordinary you may have already received it and filed it under coincidence. A dream you almost remembered, carrying the answer you fell asleep asking for. A hunch from nowhere that turned out to be right. The teaching says every call is answered, consciously made or not, and the answers come in the native language of the inner life. Sleep. Dreaming. The quiet of meditation. If you want to test it, the old instruction is disarmingly practical. Ask your question before sleep. Watch the morning.

None of this asks you to believe in visitors. Only to consider one gentle possibility. That the universe is not indifferent. That older, kinder minds are paying attention, the way you would watch a garden you planted long ago. That you have never once been entirely alone in the work of becoming.

The ancients lived inside these questions the way fish live in water. They argued about the heavens the way we argue about the weather, because the sky was the soul's weather. We have drifted, and we look up less often. That is not an accusation. It is an invitation, still open on every clear night.

And maybe, one clear night, you will feel it. Not a ship. Not a signal. Just the sense that the darkness between the stars is not empty. It is full of witnesses. And they are rooting for you.
